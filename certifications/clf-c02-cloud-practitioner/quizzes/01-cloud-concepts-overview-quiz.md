## Very Hard Questions

**20. [Scenario / Judgment] Very Hard**

> A CIO is preparing a board presentation. She must show how the cloud program's IT strategy aligns to business goals, prove the investment is delivering measurable value, and demonstrate that business risk is being managed. She is NOT discussing architecture or security controls. Which perspective is she primarily operating in — and what is the strongest clue?
>
> **A.** Platform — because she is discussing IT systems
> **B.** Governance — because she is aligning IT strategy and goals with business strategy and goals
> **C.** Security — because she mentions risk
> **D.** Operations — because she mentions delivery

**21. [Scenario / Judgment] Very Hard**

> A startup CFO argues against cloud: "Over five years, renting servers will cost more than buying them outright. The purchase price is lower than sixty months of AWS bills." What is the strongest analytical objection to her reasoning?
>
> **A.** Cloud is always cheaper than on-premises, so she is simply wrong
> **B.** She is comparing purchase price to total cost, ignoring facilities, power, labor, refresh cycles, and idle capacity
> **C.** She should use Reserved Instances, which are always the cheapest option
> **D.** Five years is too short a comparison window

**22. [Scenario / Judgment] Very Hard**

> During a migration, a company discovers that (1) its security team has no incident response runbook for cloud, (2) no one owns tracking which cloud resources exist, and (3) staff have no cloud certifications. Map these three gaps to the correct perspectives, in order.
>
> **A.** Security, Operations, People
> **B.** Operations, Platform, Governance
> **C.** Security, Governance, Platform
> **D.** People, Security, Operations

**23. [Scenario / Judgment] Very Hard**

> Two architects propose designs for the same application. Design A runs in a single Availability Zone and is cheaper. Design B spans three Availability Zones and costs more, partly due to cross-AZ data transfer charges. Which statement reflects the strongest architectural judgment?
>
> **A.** Design A is better because Cost Optimization is a Well-Architected pillar
> **B.** Design B is better because more Availability Zones is always correct
> **C.** Neither is automatically better — the right choice depends on the availability requirement, and the architect must be able to defend the trade-off
> **D.** Design A is better because cross-AZ transfer charges should always be avoided

**24. [Scenario / Judgment] Very Hard**

> A company runs a nightly data-processing job that takes about four hours, can be safely restarted if interrupted, and has no deadline before morning. They also run a customer-facing database that must be available 24/7 for the next three years. What is the most cost-effective purchasing combination?
>
> **A.** On-Demand for both
> **B.** Spot for the nightly job; Reserved/Savings Plan for the database
> **C.** Reserved for the nightly job; Spot for the database
> **D.** Dedicated Hosts for both

**25. [Scenario / Judgment] Very Hard**

> An organization completes a CAF assessment and finds strong capability in Platform and Operations, but significant gaps in People and Governance. An executive proposes: "Our technology is ready, so let's accelerate the migration and fix the people and governance issues later." Evaluate this proposal.
>
> **A.** Sound — technical readiness is the only true prerequisite for migration
> **B.** Sound — People and Governance gaps resolve naturally once systems are live
> **C.** Risky — CAF's premise is that adoption requires alignment across the WHOLE organization; unaddressed People and Governance gaps commonly stall or reverse migrations
> **D.** Risky — but only because the assessment itself is likely inaccurate
